The Envelope is your wallet. It has two pockets. Johnny tracks both.

Pocket One
FAAB

Your waiver wire dollars. Set at the start of the season by your Commissioner. Spend it on waiver wire bids or wagers. It never converts to cash. It never gets withdrawn. At the end of the season it's gone — use it or lose it. If your Commissioner designates FAAB as real money, top-ups become real obligations collected at season end.

Pocket Two
Cash

Real money. Always. You opt in and fund it yourself. Spend it on wagers. Convert it to FAAB at any time at $1 for $1. At the end of the season, whatever's left is yours to withdraw. Every top-up creates a real obligation logged in The Sheet.

The Honor System

The app is the ledger. Your Commissioner is the bank. Money moves offline. The Sheet — the season-end ledger — shows every GM exactly what they owe and what they're owed. Settlement happens at the end of the season. No surprises.

The Book

Johnny Numbers

"The Book posts Wednesday morning. Every line. Every GM. Every bet type. The number is correct."

The Book is the bet matrix. It opens Wednesday morning after the waiver wire clears. Six bet types across the top. Every GM in the league down the side. Each cell shows the current line for that bet against that opponent.

Odds are powered by a Monte Carlo simulation engine running 10,000 scenarios. They update live as lineups change. A trend arrow shows you whether the line moved in your favor since Wednesday open.

Bet Type 1
Moneyline

Who wins the matchup outright. Pick your side. Higher projected score wins. Straight win or loss — no margin required.

Bet Type 2
Spread

The favorite has to win by more than the spread. The underdog wins the bet by losing within it. Same logic as football — applied to fantasy points.

Bet Type 3
Over / Under

Both teams' combined fantasy score goes over or under the posted total. You're not picking a winner — you're picking a number.

Bet Type 4
Player Prop

Pick one player from either roster. Bet their fantasy score goes over or under a posted number. Not available if the player is ineligible or inactive. The Brain will tell you before you commit.

Bet Type 5
Bench Battle

Each GM's top four eligible bench players go head to head. Active or Questionable only — no IR, no OUT designations. Not available if either GM can't field four eligible bench players.

Bet Type 6
The Parlay

Three positional matchups in one bet — TE vs TE, DEF vs DEF, K vs K. Win two of three legs to cash. One loss doesn't kill it. Two losses do.

Important

Beefs transcend the weekly matchup. You can challenge any GM in the league on any bet type, any week — not just your current opponent.

05

How Betting Works

Putting Out a Contract is how you start a beef. Here's how the action moves from challenge to settlement.

  • Step 1 Putting Out a Contract. Open The Book, tap your opponent, pick your bet type and stake. Add trash talk if you feel like it. Challenge sent. Their clock starts.
  • Step 2 Their move. Accept, counter the stake, or pass. One counter maximum. Denomination and bet type are locked — stake only. No re-counter.
  • Step 3 The Handshake. Bet accepted. Odds lock at the exact line at the moment of acceptance — not when you issued the challenge. Stakes go into escrow. Bet posts to the League Feed.
  • Step 4 Lineup Watch. Before you accept any challenge, check the line. A GM playing possum has a suboptimal lineup in place — maybe a injured starter, maybe a weak bench fill-in. Could be deliberate to draw in favorable odds. Could be innocent — no better option available. The app can't tell the difference and doesn't try. What it does: surfaces the current lineup, shows you the odds it produced, and tells you what happens if that lineup changes after you accept. The Adjustment handles the rest.
  • Step 5 The Adjustment. Yahoo projections update constantly. Lineups change. A starter gets scratched Saturday night. A GM optimizes Sunday morning. Every change shifts the real probability of each side winning. The Adjustment reprices stakes automatically when this happens — against the original pot ceiling set at The Handshake. Neither GM ever owes more than they committed. If your side got worse, you get a partial refund. The pot shrinks. Nobody gets squeezed.
  • Step 6 Whistle Blows. First involved player kicks off. Bet locks. No more adjustments. The Skipper doesn't want to hear about it.
  • Step 7 Collections. Games end. Claude settles automatically. Commissioner reviews and confirms. Winner's Envelope credited. Tuesday morning. No negotiating.
Possum Watch

Playing possum means fielding a lineup that looks weaker than it needs to be — pulling in favorable odds, then optimizing before kickoff. Deliberate or innocent, The Adjustment neutralizes it. Odds reprice when the lineup improves. Stakes adjust. The edge disappears. The Handshake protects both sides.

06

The Adjustment
How the Odds Stay Fair

Johnny Numbers

"The Handshake locks the deal. The Adjustment keeps it honest. Nobody owes more than they put in. The number is correct."

Fantasy Beefs bets run from Wednesday morning to Monday night. A lot happens between The Handshake and the Whistle. Yahoo projections update. Starters get scratched. GMs optimize their lineups. Every change shifts the real probability of each side winning — which means the odds you accepted on Thursday may not reflect the game being played on Sunday.

Without a correction mechanism, the GM who accepted unfavorable odds is stuck with them. They took -150 when the real line was -110. The other GM benefited from stale information. That is not a fair bet. That is an information asymmetry exploit. The Adjustment closes that gap.

Why Odds Shift
Projections Update

Yahoo projects player and team scores throughout the week. A starter gets ruled out Saturday night. A backup gets elevated. Weather changes the over/under. Every update changes the Monte Carlo simulation output — which changes the line.

Why Odds Shift
Lineups Change

A GM optimizes their lineup Sunday morning. Swaps a questionable starter for a healthy bench player. That changes their projected score — which changes the odds — which changes what a fair bet looks like. The Adjustment catches it.

Special Case
Playing Possum

A GM fields a suboptimal lineup — deliberately or not. Odds favor the other side. Bets come in at those favorable odds. GM optimizes before kickoff. Without The Adjustment, the betting GM got played. With it, the odds reprice the moment the lineup improves and stakes adjust. The edge disappears either way.

How it works mechanically:

  • At Handshake Both stakes go into escrow. The combined pot is the ceiling — it never grows beyond this number. Ever.
  • Odds shift Monte Carlo reruns. New fair probabilities calculated. Stakes recalculate proportionally against the original pot ceiling.
  • If your side got worse Your required stake drops. The difference is refunded to your Envelope immediately. You never owe more than you committed.
  • If your side got better Your required stake increases — but only up to what you originally put in. The ceiling holds. No surprises.
  • Pot shrinks After refunds, the total pot is smaller than at The Handshake. The winner takes the adjusted pot. The loser got their refund already.
  • Whistle Blows First player kicks off. Adjustments stop. Whatever the stakes are at kickoff — that's the bet. Final.
The Fairness Guarantee

The Handshake locks the deal. The Adjustment keeps it honest as reality changes. You never owe more than you put in. You never get stuck with odds that no longer reflect the game being played. This is what makes peer-to-peer betting in Our Thing different from a handshake bet on a napkin.

Appendix

The Math
How the Numbers Work

Fantasy Beefs runs on the same mathematics used by every legitimate bookmaker. Nothing invented. Nothing proprietary. This appendix shows the formulas, where they come from, how they connect to each tab in the Odds Calculator, and how they tie out to accepted industry practice.

Johnny Numbers

"The number is correct. It was correct before you questioned it. But since you asked — here is exactly how it was derived."

A.1 · Calculator Tab 1 & 2
American Odds

American odds are the standard format used by every US sportsbook. They come in two forms — negative for the favorite, positive for the underdog.

Negative line (favorite): How much you must wager to win $100. A line of -150 means bet $150 to win $100. The favorite wins more often — so you risk more to win less.

Positive line (underdog): How much you win on a $100 wager. A line of +130 means bet $100 to win $130. The underdog wins less often — so you risk less to win more.

Even money: A 50% probability returns +100, not -100. Even money is expressed as a positive line. Fantasy Beefs follows this standard.

The Formula

Probability → American Odds
Favorite (p > 0.5):  line = −(p ÷ (1 − p)) × 100
Underdog (p < 0.5):  line = ((1 − p) ÷ p) × 100
Even (p = 0.5):  line = +100

Example: 60% win probability → −(0.60 ÷ 0.40) × 100 = −150

A.2 · Calculator Tab 2
Implied Probability

Every American odds line implies a probability. This is the market's estimate of how likely that outcome is — baked into the price. Running it in reverse reveals what probability the line is actually pricing.

The Formula

American Odds → Implied Probability
Negative line:  p = (−line) ÷ (−line + 100)
Positive line:  p = 100 ÷ (line + 100)

Example: −150 → 150 ÷ 250 = 60.0%
Example: +130 → 100 ÷ 230 = 43.5%

60.0% + 43.5% = 103.5% — that 3.5% gap is the vig.

A.3 · Calculator Tab 4
Vig / Overround

Vig — also called juice or overround — is the bookmaker's margin. It is built symmetrically into both sides of a market so the book profits regardless of which side wins. The sum of implied probabilities across both sides always exceeds 100% when vig is present.

The Formula

Overround = (implied_A + implied_B) − 1.0
Vig % = overround ÷ (implied_A + implied_B) × 100

Example: −150 / +130 market
implied_A = 60.0%, implied_B = 43.5%, sum = 103.5%
overround = 0.035
vig % = 0.035 ÷ 1.035 × 100 = 3.38%

Standard US sportsbook: 4–5% vig. Sharp books: ~2%. Fantasy Beefs default: 0%.

A.4 · Calculator Tab 2 & 4
Devigging — Fair Probability

Devigging strips the bookmaker's margin out of a posted market to reveal the fair probability underneath. The industry-standard method for two-outcome markets is simple normalization — divide each implied probability by their sum. The result is two probabilities that sum exactly to 1.0. That invariant — sum to 1 — is the mathematical guarantee that the market is fair.

The Formula

Simple Normalization (sum-to-1)
fair_A = implied_A ÷ (implied_A + implied_B)
fair_B = implied_B ÷ (implied_A + implied_B)

Example: −150 / +130 market
implied_A = 0.600, implied_B = 0.435, sum = 1.035
fair_A = 0.600 ÷ 1.035 = 57.97%
fair_B = 0.435 ÷ 1.035 = 42.03%
Sum = 100.00% ✓

Fantasy Beefs uses simple normalization as the canonical devig method. The Shin method — which accounts for favorite-longshot bias — is available as a future option. Monte Carlo simulation produces win probabilities directly from simulated matchups, so devigging only applies when evaluating external lines or when The Juice is enabled by the Commissioner.

A.5 · Calculator Tab 3
Spread Lines & Over/Under

A spread line expresses the projected margin of victory as a wagerable number. An Over/Under line expresses the combined projected score of both teams. Both are derived directly from projected scores and constructed so both sides of the bet land near 50/50 probability.

The Formula

Spread Line = floor(|score_A − score_B|) + 0.5
Over/Under Line = floor(score_A + score_B) + 0.5

The +0.5 hook eliminates the possibility of a push. Both sides land at ~50/50.

Example: projected scores 142.3 vs 118.7
Spread = floor(23.6) + 0.5 = 23.5 (favorite −23.5, underdog +23.5)
O/U = floor(261.0) + 0.5 = 261.5

The hook (+0.5) is standard bookmaking construction used universally in sports betting to prevent push outcomes on spread and total bets. Fantasy Beefs follows this convention exactly.

A.6 · Calculator Tab 5
Flexible Stake and Return with Max Stake Ceiling

This is Fantasy Beefs' proprietary escrow mechanic — the engine behind The Adjustment. It has a direct parallel in legitimate bookmaking: the mark-to-market cash-out model used by Betfair and major sportsbooks, which allows bettors to settle positions early at the current market price.

The core principle: stakes are sized against fair devigged probabilities, not posted odds. The pot established at The Handshake is the ceiling — it never grows. When odds shift, stakes recalculate proportionally. The GM on the improving side never gets asked for more than they committed. The GM on the declining side gets a partial refund. The invariant that must hold at every point: fair probabilities sum to 1.0.

The Formula

At The Handshake:
pot = stake_A + stake_B  ← ceiling, never exceeded

On odds shift:
new_stake_A = pot × new_fair_prob_B
new_stake_B = pot × new_fair_prob_A

If new_stake_A < escrow_A → refund difference to GM A
If new_stake_B < escrow_B → refund difference to GM B

Invariant: new_fair_prob_A + new_fair_prob_B = 1.0 always

Example: original pot $17.25, odds shift from 60/40 to 55/45
new_stake_A = 17.25 × 0.45 = $7.76
new_stake_B = 17.25 × 0.55 = $9.49
GM B receives refund: $10.35 − $9.49 = $0.86
new_pot = $7.76 + $9.49 = $17.25

The industry parallel: a Betfair exchange bettor who lays off a position at the current market price is doing the same thing — repricing their exposure against current probability. Fantasy Beefs automates this for both sides simultaneously, enforces the ceiling, and guarantees neither GM is ever asked for more than they committed at The Handshake. This is what makes peer-to-peer wagering mathematically fair across a week of changing information.
